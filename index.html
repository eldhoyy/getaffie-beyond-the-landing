EVERY LANDING LEAVES A STORY.
01 / THE IDEA

Not everything returns home.

A spacecraft landing is a carefully choreographed sequence. To deliver a rover or lander safely, missions rely on protective shells, parachutes, descent engines and structural hardware. Many of these components complete their job and remain where they fall. They are not forgotten failures: they are part of the engineering story.

03 / OBJECT ARCHIVE

What remains behind?

Explore examples of equipment associated with the Perseverance landing. Filter by mission phase.

ENTRY · MARS 2020

Heat shield

Protected the rover and descent system from intense heating during atmospheric entry. The shield separated before parachute deployment.

ENTRY · MARS 2020

Backshell

Part of the aeroshell that enclosed the rover during cruise and entry. It carried the parachute and helped protect the vehicle.

DESCENT · MARS 2020

Supersonic parachute

Deployed to slow the spacecraft dramatically in the thin Martian atmosphere before powered descent.

DESCENT · MARS 2020

Descent stage

The powered sky-crane platform lowered Perseverance to the surface, then flew away to a separate impact location.

SURFACE · APOLLO

Lunar Module descent stage

The lower stage of the Apollo Lunar Module remained on the Moon after astronauts lifted off in the ascent stage.

SURFACE · MARS 2020

Landing-site debris field

Separated landing hardware can be observed around the landing region, offering evidence of the entry, descent and landing sequence.

04 / TWO WORLDS

Moon and Mars: different worlds, shared traces

THE MOON

Apollo landing legacy

The Apollo Lunar Module used a two-stage design. The descent stage provided landing propulsion and a platform; it stayed on the lunar surface when the ascent stage departed.

MARS

Robotic precision

Mars 2020 used an aeroshell, parachute and rocket-powered sky crane to deliver Perseverance. Several components separated and remained on the planet.
